How to fix your entire life in one day

Resolutions fail 80–90% of the time because people change their actions and never touch the thing underneath them — who they believe they are. Seven ideas, then a one-day pen-and-paper protocol.

Reformatted for internal use · animations are CSS & SVG, no external assets
Idea I

You aren't there because you aren't the person who'd be there

A resolution has two requirements. People only ever do the first.

  • 2Change your actions toward the goal. Second-order, least important — the part people white-knuckle for three weeks.
  • 1Change who you are so behaviour just follows. The bodybuilder doesn't grind to eat well — he'd have to grind to eat badly.

Build a great life on a rotting foundation and it collapses back to baseline by February. You must own the lifestyle that creates the outcome long before the outcome arrives — and keep it afterward.

What this section means

Stop asking "what habits do I need?" Ask "what kind of person has these by default?" — then become that first. Habits that don't serve the new identity should start to feel disgusting, because you can now see what they compound into.

Idea II

You aren't there because you don't actually want to be

Trust only movement. Life happens at the level of events, not of words.— Alfred Adler

All behaviour is goal-oriented — but most goals are unconscious, and some quietly harm you while sounding respectable.

  • A"I procrastinate because I lack discipline" → real goal: avoid the judgment of finishing and shipping.
  • B"I stay in the dead-end job because I'm not a risk-taker" → real goal: safety, predictability, not looking like a failure to people who also stayed.

A goal isn't a to-do line. A goal is a lens — it decides which information, ideas and resources you even notice. Real change means swapping the lens, not the checklist.

What this section means

Before setting any resolution, name the unconscious payoff of your current behaviour. If you can't say what staying stuck is protecting, your hidden goal will quietly override the shiny one.

Idea III

You aren't there because you're afraid to be there

Identity is a loop that has run on you since childhood. Each pass hardens it.

Once physical survival is handled (easy today), you start surviving on the ideological level — protecting and reproducing your mind. Challenge a belief someone is fused with and they feel physically slapped. Same mechanism whether it's a politics, a religion, or seeing yourself as "a gamer" who wouldn't do the harder thing.

What this section means

The block isn't laziness — it's a defended self. Change requires letting a version of you die, and paying the social cost of no longer being that person. Say the identity statement out loud: "I am the type of person who…"

Idea IV

The life you want lives at a specific level of mind

The mind evolves through predictable stages (Maslow, Loevinger / Cook-Greuter, Spiral Dynamics, Koe's own Human 3.0). The 80/20 of nine ego stages:

  • 3Conformist — you are your group; its rules feel like reality.
  • 4Self-Aware — you notice an inner life that doesn't match the outside, but don't know what to do with it.
  • 5Conscientious — you build your own principles and hold yourself to them.
  • 6Individualist — you see your principles were shaped by context, and hold them loosely.
  • 7Strategist — you work systems while watching your own blind spots.

What this section means

Your stage isn't the point — the pattern of moving between stages is, and it's the same every time: tension → disorientation → reorganisation at a higher level. The protocol runs that pattern on purpose.

Idea V

Intelligence is the ability to get what you want out of life

The only real test of intelligence is if you get what you want out of life.— Naval Ravikant

Three ingredients: agency (likelihood you act), opportunity (worth of the goal you can reach — digital abundance is right there), and intelligence (ability to convert the other two). Read through cybernetics — Greek kybernetikos, "good at steering" — intelligence is a feedback loop:

Most people's goals were assigned: go to school, get the job, get offended, play victim, retire at 65 — a known path that doesn't work. To get smarter: reject the known path, dive into the unknown, set higher goals, embrace the chaos, study nature's general principles, become a deep generalist.

What this section means

Treat your life like a control system. Pick a real target, make a move, measure honestly, adjust, repeat. The only true failure mode is refusing to learn from the last loop.

Why goals here aren't normal goals

A goal is a point of view you swap into to enter the right state for the next action — not a finish line. There is no finish line; enjoyment is in the progress.

The concentric circles become who you are

Apply it · 16 years in tech

16 years in. AI engineering. UK. Target: a frontier lab.

The essay's claim: you don't get to Anthropic or OpenAI by applying harder. You become the person a frontier lab already wants, then the offer follows. Framework applied — not insider hiring advice.

Idea I → applied

Be the hire before you're hired

The labs don't hire "a senior engineer with 16 YOE." They hire someone visibly already doing frontier-shaped work — evals, inference & performance, interpretability tooling, agent harnesses, red-teaming — in public.

The tell from the essay: if reading a paper, writing, or building in public feels like grind you force yourself into, you don't have the lifestyle that produces the outcome. Someone in that job does those by default — they'd have to grind to stop.

  • +16 YOE helps: you own ambiguous, underspecified problems and lead without hand-holding.
  • −16 YOE traps: the identity "I'm past proving myself." You'll share rooms with people who've published since their PhD. Proof-of-work resets.

The move

Aim to be someone who gets a warm referral from inside — not someone optimising a cold application.

Idea II → applied

Check the hidden goal

"I want to work at a frontier lab" — now watch the behaviour. Are your evenings already going to evals, model-behaviour writeups, PRs to repos the labs read (vLLM, transformers, the labs' SDKs, MCP servers)? If not, the stated goal may be serving a hidden one: prestige, a cleaner escape from the current job, external validation.

  • AAnthropic — safety framing, research-adjacent product, an unusually heavy writing culture. If you can't write clearly, that's the gap.
  • OOpenAI — scale, shipping velocity, huge product surface. Bias toward people who move fast and ship.

The move

If the real goal is "leave my current job," a frontier lab is a slow, low-odds route. Name it — your answer to "why this lab" is itself interview signal.

Idea III → applied

The identity that has to die

  • 1"I'm an implementer, not a researcher." Labs hire strong engineers into research-facing roles. The wall is self-imposed.
  • 2"16 YOE = senior = I skip the proof-of-work." You don't.
  • 3"Posting in public is cringe / hype-chasing." Shipping evals and being wrong in front of peers is the social cost the essay says you must pay.
  • 4Going from big-fish to relative beginner in a room of specialists.

Write the sentence

"I am the type of person who ships public work on frontier-AI problems every week, and talks to the people building them." Then notice what it costs you socially to become that.

Ideas IV + V → applied

Swap the lens, then run the loop

Move off the assigned ladder (hit milestones, effort → reward, wait to be promoted) onto the Strategist lens: play the actual hiring system — referrals + visible artefacts + being known to people inside, not LeetCode plus cold apply.

  • GGoal: final-round or offer at a frontier lab within 12 months (London — both have offices, no move needed).
  • AAct: ship one artefact.
  • SSense: recruiter replies, referral offers, callback rate, repo stars, whether insiders reply to your DMs.
  • CCompare & adjust: one rejection is not a stop. Change the artefact, the pitch, the target team, the referrer — and loop.

Apply it · starting fresh

New to tech, aiming at an AI engineering job

The essay says no credential is the gate and "applying harder" isn't the lever. Become someone who already builds and ships AI things in public — the junior offer follows. Framework applied, not a syllabus.

Idea I → applied

Be a builder before you're hired

Entry AI engineering today ≈ a software engineer who's fluent with LLM APIs, retrieval, evals, and prompt / agent design — and can ship. SWE fundamentals still decide most interviews; the LLM layer sits on top.

Junior offers go to people with a visible trail: 3–4 deployed projects, real commit history, short writeups. Not certificates. The essay's tell: if building only happens when you force it — and mostly you watch tutorials — you don't have the identity yet. Someone in that job builds because they can't not.

The move

Become the candidate a hiring manager can literally see working: deployed links, a README, a build log of what broke.

Idea II → applied

Check the hidden goal

"I want an AI engineering job." Now watch the behaviour. Are you shipping with the APIs every week, or collecting courses? Tutorial-hell is often an unconscious goal — feel productive, avoid the judgment of shipping something real and flawed.

  • ?Do you want AI specifically, or a stable, well-paid tech job? Both are fine — but they lead to different plans and different applications. Say which.
  • ?Is the next course genuinely the missing piece, or the place you go to feel safe instead of shipping?

The move

Finish one real, deployed project before buying the next course. The course is usually the hiding place.

Idea III → applied

The identity that has to die

  • 1"I'm not technical / not a real engineer yet." You become one by shipping, publicly, before you feel ready.
  • 2"One more course and then I'll start." There is no ready.
  • 3"I'll build in public once I'm good enough." Being visibly mid is exactly the cost the essay says to pay.
  • 4"I'm too late / too old to switch." That's the protocol's "most embarrassing reason" question — answer it honestly.

Write the sentence

"I'm someone who ships a small AI project every two weeks and writes up what broke." Then notice what it costs you socially to say that out loud.

Ideas IV + V → applied

Run your own system, then run the loop

Move off Conformist (follow the assigned curriculum, wait for a credential to grant permission) to running your own system: learn what your project needs, judge your own output, set your own milestones. The credential is not the gate — a portfolio is.

Your opportunity capital is low right now, so loop volume matters most.

  • GGoal: first paid AI-adjacent role in ~9–12 months — junior AI engineer, LLM-focused SWE, AI product engineer, applied-AI at a non-tech company, forward-deployed, AI dev-rel.
  • AAct: ship a small thing.
  • SSense: replies, interview invites, GitHub stars, feedback on posts, referral offers.
  • CAdjust: widen the targets, rewrite the résumé to the job description, swap the project.
